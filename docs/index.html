How Raycast's Review Loop Leaks Extensions

PRs to the Raycast Store more than doubled between 2024 and 2026, the same period coding agents went mainstream. PRs to existing extensions still merge quickly. New extensions increasingly stall: most contributors respond to the first review comments, often Greptile's, then stop after the next round of review. Others sit as drafts that were never marked ready, or wait weeks for one of the two maintainers who review them.

This report follows that loop (open a PR, get review comments, push fixes, get re-reviewed) and shows where contributors drop out of it. As an author of 18 original extensions and contributor to 23 others, I have substantial first-hand knowledge of this loop.

Independent analysis by Chris Messina. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Raycast.

What to change

Five changes to make, ordered by cost-benefit ratio

Each is sized against 2026-H1, when 1,529 new-extension submissions were opened and 572 merged.

1

Put Greptile's rules in the repo

Reach: Every PRCost: Low

Move Greptile's custom rules into a .greptile/ folder in raycast/extensions, Greptile's recommended setup: version-controlled, reviewed in PRs, readable by contributors and their agents. Ship the deterministic rules as a local check.

Trade-off. The rules become public, so people can write to them; rule changes go through PR review, which adds process and an audit trail.

2

Replace the PR checklist with checks

Reach: Every PRCost: Low

The five checkboxes are the contributor's self-attestations. Two are file checks a script can verify, and the build half of a third is already run by CI. Move those to CI and keep what only the contributor knows: a Store search, a promise to mark ready, testing the build in Raycast, which platforms they tested on (macOS, Windows, or both), a screencast, and whether they can respond to review. If the contributor couldn't test on one platform, a needs Windows tester (or macOS) label asks a volunteer to try it there.

Trade-off. CI checks need upkeep; the checkboxes cost nothing to maintain, but nothing enforces them.

3

Make the draft step impossible to miss

Reach: 1 in 6 new submissionsCost: Low

In 267 of 1,529 submissions, the PR was never marked ready. Have raycastbot comment on a quiet draft that review starts when it's marked ready, print the same in ray publish output, and stop automatic Greptile reviews on drafts (#32497, triggerOnDrafts: false) so marking it ready triggers one clean Greptile review.

Trade-off. Drafts lose automatic early feedback; contributors who want it can ask Greptile with a comment.

4

Steer duplicates before they're built

Reach: 1 in 4 new submissionsCost: Medium

In 353 of 1,529 submissions, a maintainer or Greptile told the contributor a similar extension already exists, and redirects rarely turn into contributions: of 460 unmerged submissions across the window where a reviewer linked a specific Store extension, 33 (7%) were followed by a merged PR from the same contributor to that extension. Move the search earlier: show matching Store extensions when a contributor starts a new extension, and have a bot ask what they want to change and propose how to add it to the existing extension first. If the need is different enough, the submission passes that gate and proceeds as a new extension. The docs would also need to cover how to extend someone else's extension for an unusual need, which today takes back-and-forth and deep platform knowledge.

Trade-off. A step before every new submission, which contributors can read as a barrier; owners of existing extensions get more PRs to review. Transferring ownership is the other route, but it's delicate when the current owner is still around.

5

Pause the stale clock while Raycast reviews

Reach: 368 stale-closed after reviewCost: Medium

Raycast's maintainers are the gate, so time a PR spends waiting on a maintainer shouldn't count against the contributor. When maintainer review slows (a maintainer is away, the queue spikes), the stale bot closes PRs for inactivity that isn't the contributor's. Of 368 submissions the stale bot closed after a maintainer reply, in 271 the contributor had responded to earlier review comments and a reviewer commented last; in 185 of those, the contributor's last commit or comment came before the first maintainer reply. They addressed Greptile's comments, waited, and didn't come back after the maintainer's review. Have raycastbot apply an awaiting review label when the contributor pushes after review comments, exempt it from the stale bot, and restart the clock when a maintainer replies. Pair it with a complete checklist in the maintainer's first review, so it's the last round.

Trade-off. Paused PRs can pile up in the maintainers' queue. A clock that always runs makes contributors shepherd their work forward; pausing only while the PR waits on a maintainer keeps that pressure on the contributor's turns.

Volume

Twice the PRs, more than twice the new extensions

2024 was flat at about 300 PRs a month. Growth starts in late 2025 and holds through 2026: 6,418 PRs in the first nine and a half months of 2026, against 3,594 in all of 2024. New-extension submissions grew fastest, from 1,021 in 2024 to 2,420 so far in 2026.

September 2026 is the busiest month in the data (889 PRs), the first full month after Raycast 2.0 launched on August 25. Volume also rose after Windows opened to extension developers in September 2025 and around the Windows public beta in November. The data shows the timing; it can't show what caused it.

Outcomes

PRs to existing extensions still merge. New extensions increasingly don't.

PRs to existing extensions merge about 80–92% of the time across the whole window. For new extensions the merge rate fell from 57% to 37%, and the share the stale bot closed nearly tripled, from 16% to 43%.

Contributors closing their own submissions held at 16–27% through 2026-Q2 (resubmissions counted once). Closures by anyone else (maintainers, extension owners or other accounts) stayed under 10% in every quarter. The growth is almost entirely in stale-bot closes.

The 2026-Q3 drop to 24% is partly the stale bot's longer clock (25 + 7 days from June 2) and partly that September's PRs haven't had time to go stale. October is too new to read.

Why they close

Inside the loop: why new submissions get auto-closed

New submissions the stale bot closed fall into three groups. In 2026, nearly two thirds had a maintainer reply first (503 of 799: 211 of 338 in Q1, 157 of 286 in Q2, 135 of 175 in Q3). In most of those the contributor had responded to earlier review comments (often Greptile's) and a reviewer commented last: 143 of the 211 in Q1, 128 of 157 in Q2. Cases where the contributor pushed or replied last and was waiting on a reviewer were small (9–26 a quarter in 2026).

About a quarter were never marked ready for review (22–39% of stale-bot closes each quarter since 2025-Q2). ray publish opens the PR as a draft, and review starts when it's marked ready; maintainers only occasionally comment on drafts (none of 2026-H1's 267 never-ready submissions got a maintainer reply). Through February 2025, contributors opened 1–8% of PRs a month as drafts; from 2025-Q2 through 2026-Q3 it's 57–68% a quarter. 17–24% of new submissions from 2025-Q2 through 2026-Q2 were never marked ready, against under 1% in 2024. The stale bot doesn't exempt drafts.

The rest were marked ready and never got a comment or review from a maintainer: 36 in 2026-Q1 and 58 in 2026-Q2, when maintainer review was slowest. Many more PRs are drafts at close than were never ready: maintainers often convert a PR back to draft while they wait for fixes.

Velocity

Once ready, new extensions waited three weeks in the first half of 2026

Existing-extension PRs got faster: median time to merge fell from 1.5 days to under one, and the first reply from a maintainer or the extension's owner comes within a day, often within hours.

New extensions, measured from when the PR was marked ready for review (or from creation, for PRs opened ready), waited a median of 4.8 days for a maintainer's first comment or review in 2024-Q1 and 22 days in 2026-Q2, among PRs that got a reply. In that quarter 66% of ready new submissions had no maintainer reply within 21 days, the stale bot's clock until June 2. In 2026-Q3 the median fell back to 12 days and that share to 10%.

For two years the queue was one maintainer deep: pernielsentikaer made 99% of maintainer review actions in 2024 and 95% in 2025. In 2026 a second maintainer, 0xdhrv, made 63% of them.

Greptile

Greptile arrived in February 2025 and has reviewed nearly everything since

Greptile's first review was on February 17, 2025 (#17128). It reviewed 48% of PRs opened in 2025-Q1 (it also reviewed some January PRs late), about 85% from 2025-Q2 through 2026-Q2, and 96–100% since July 2026. On new PRs since July 2026 its median time to first review is under 10 minutes.

Its mean confidence score rose from 3.3 in 2025-Q3, when it started publishing one, to about 4.4 out of 5 in mid-2026. Either contributors' PRs got cleaner or Greptile's scale moved; the data can't separate the two. Review rounds aren't charted: Greptile edits one summary comment in place, so contributors' pushes after the first review leave no separate record.

Friction

Authors answer the first round. The second one loses them.

The share of submissions with review comments that closed unmerged without the contributor ever replying fell from about 12% in 2024 to about 4% in 2026. The share that closed unmerged for any reason stayed in a similar range: 27–34% across 2024, 24–31% from 2025-Q2 through 2026-Q2. These describe what happened, not why; the data can't show whether Greptile's comments changed anyone's mind.

What grew is the pattern in the section above: a contributor responds to the first review comments, often Greptile's, and doesn't come back after the next review. With maintainer replies taking three weeks in early 2026, each extra round costs most of the stale bot's window.

Extension owners leave less of the review feedback over time (15% of submissions in 2024, 6–8% in 2026), because more of the volume is new extensions, which have no owner yet.

What Greptile flags

About a quarter of what Greptile flags is mechanical

The data holds 36,571 inline review comments (findings) from Greptile on 7,625 PRs. That's a floor: a few PRs with very long review threads were truncated at collection. Clustered by keyword, the biggest themes are behavior bugs specific to the extension, cleanup, CHANGELOG format and error handling.

27% of findings fall in mechanical categories: CHANGELOG format, screenshots, hand-typed preferences, package.json fields, lint config, dependencies and localization. On new extensions their contributors abandoned, it's 31%. Findings Greptile itself tagged as logic or P0/P1 are excluded from the mechanical set. 67% of PRs with findings got at least one mechanical finding, and 17% got only mechanical ones. This is a keyword classification, not a hand-validated one.

Each mechanical theme is a rule Greptile applies the same way across PRs. Raycast's public Copilot rubric fully covers two of those themes: CHANGELOG format and localization.

The decision

Should Raycast publish the Greptile rules?

Yes, as a check contributors run, with clear expectations about what it fixes. It addresses the mechanical quarter of findings, and every round it saves matters when a maintainer reply can take three weeks. It doesn't fix the review wait or the draft step.

For publishing the rules

  • 27% of findings, and 31% on abandoned new extensions, fall in mechanical categories. A local check could catch many before the first push instead of one round later.
  • Most stale-bot closes after a maintainer reply are contributors who did one round of fixes and stopped after the next review. Fewer rounds means fewer chances to stop.
  • It runs today. The deterministic rules already run locally in about 40 ms in preflight.mjs, calibrated against 35 real extensions. Greptile supports committing rules to a .greptile/ folder, so the review bot and contributors would read the same file. A contributor has already opened a .greptile/config.json PR (#32498) for the draft setting.
  • Coding agents follow a rules file they can read. They can't follow rules they can't see.

Against, or not enough

  • A document alone didn't move the numbers. After the Copilot rubric was published on August 29, 2025, the CHANGELOG share of Greptile findings rose (5.5% to 12.0%, by finding date) instead of falling.
  • A quarter of the new extensions the stale bot closed were never marked ready. No rules file fixes that.
  • Behavior bugs, security and judgment calls make up most findings. No rules file catches those.
  • Raycast would have to keep a public copy in sync with Greptile's config.

Recommendation

Publish the rules as a script contributors run before pushing, next to the rubric. Expect it to cut some review rounds, not to fix dropout on its own.

Then shorten the loop after a maintainer's review. The largest group of stale-bot closes on new extensions is contributors who responded to earlier review comments and didn't come back after the next review. A complete checklist in the maintainer's first review, so it is the last round, matters more than any single rule.

Fix the draft step. In a quarter of stale-bot closes, the PR was never marked ready. A raycastbot comment on a quiet draft explaining that review starts when it is marked ready, or a line at the end of ray publish output, costs little.

Watch the clock. In 2026-Q2, 66% of ready new submissions had no maintainer reply within 21 days. Pausing the stale bot while a contributor is waiting on a maintainer would stop closures for inactivity that isn't the contributor's. The June 2026 move to 25 + 7 days was a step in that direction.

A checklist worth checking

Running npm run publish (which calls ray publish) opens the Store PR with Raycast's pull request template. The Today card is that template's checklist: five boxes the contributor ticks on every PR, which nothing verifies. These days it adds little compared with what a checklist could catch. The Proposed card is an alternative.

Today

  • I read the extension guidelines
  • I read the documentation about publishing
  • I ran npm run build and tested this distribution build in Raycast
  • I checked that files in the assets folder are used by the extension itself
  • I checked that assets used in the README are located outside the metadata folder if they were not generated with our metadata tool

All five are the contributor's self-attestations. Two say "I read the docs." The third bundles a build, which CI already runs, with testing in Raycast, which only a person can do. The last two are file checks a script can do. None address what loses new extensions in this data: drafts never marked ready, duplicates, and the review loop.

Proposed

  • New extension? I searched the Store for this service. If something similar exists, I explain below why this should be separate (different platform, unmaintained, different scope), or I'm contributing to it instead.
  • I tested the production build in Raycast.
  • I tested on macOS / Windows. If I couldn't test on both, I've asked for a tester on the other platform below, and raycastbot adds a needs Windows tester or needs macOS tester label.
  • I'll mark this PR Ready for review when it's done. Drafts aren't reviewed. A PR with no activity for 25 days is marked stale and closed 7 days later.
  • I attached a screencast or screenshots of what changed.
  • I can respond to review within two weeks, or I've said so below.

Move to CI, no checkbox: build and lint, the deterministic Greptile rules (CHANGELOG format, screenshots, generated preference types, package.json fields), unused files in assets, README asset paths (allowing images generated by the metadata tool).

Method

How this was measured

Every PR opened on raycast/extensions from January 1, 2024 to October 18, 2026 (15,078 PRs), collected through GitHub GraphQL with timelines, reviews and comments. Monthly totals match the reference counts for January 2024 – June 2025 exactly. 12 bot-authored PRs are excluded. Connections were capped (50 reviews, 30 comments per review, 100 comments per PR), so a handful of very long threads are incomplete.

Auto-closed
closed unmerged by raycastbot, with the status: stalled label or the inactivity message. raycastbot also closes every merged PR, so the closer alone isn't enough. 1,932 PRs. GitHub's live search finds 2,030 closed PRs with the stalled label: 1,907 of them are in that set, and 123 were closed by a person while stalled. The other 25 auto-closes lost the label afterward.
Submission
new-extension PRs from the same author for the same folder are one submission, ending at its first merge and scored by its last PR; a package.json added to a folder that already exists is an update.
Feedback
a Greptile review, or a non-approval review or comment from a Raycast maintainer (COLLABORATOR or MEMBER), the extension owner (named in raycastbot's 🔔 ping), or another contributor requesting changes, before the PR closed, across every PR in the submission.
Contributor replied
a comment, review or force-push by the PR's author, or a commit authored by them.
Ready
replayed from draft transitions; a PR that opened ready counts from creation, one that opened as a draft from its first ready-for-review event. A submission is ever-ready if any of its PRs was.
Maintainer reply (human reply in the data)
a comment or review from a maintainer or the extension's owner. For new extensions that's a maintainer: owners appear on 10 of 5,948 new-extension PRs.
